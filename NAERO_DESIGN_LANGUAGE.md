# PART I: WHY NAERO DESERVES ITS OWN VISUAL LANGUAGE

Every year, millions of people leave everything they know. Their language. Their streets. Their family. Their sense of belonging. They arrive somewhere new and face a question that has no simple answer: *How do I live here?*

Naero exists to answer that question.

Not with a search engine. Not with a directory. Not with a list of tips. With a presence. A companion that walks beside you as you build a new life.

This is not a utility. This is not a tool. This is a relationship between a person and a guide. And that relationship needs a visual language as unique as the experience it serves.

## Why Generic Design Languages Fail Naero

Material Design was built for apps. Human Interface Guidelines were built for platforms. Neither was built for *this* — a product that must simultaneously guide, warm, comfort, motivate, and accompany someone through one of the most disorienting experiences of their life.

A generic design language gives you buttons, cards, and navigation patterns. Naero needs more. Naero needs:

- **A visual voice** that speaks warmth without words
- **A spatial philosophy** that creates belonging through layout
- **An emotional architecture** that transforms anxiety into confidence
- **A presence system** that makes the AI feel alive, not mechanical
- **A trust framework** that makes a stranger feel like a friend

No existing design language provides these. Naero must create its own.

## The Category Naero Inhabits

Naero is not competing with Google Maps, Google Translate, LinkedIn, or ChatGPT. Each of those is a tool. Naero is a *companion*. The closest comparisons:

**Headspace** proved that calm could be a product philosophy. But Headspace is about internal states — meditation, breathing, mental health. Naero is about external navigation — bureaucracy, housing, healthcare, community. The user doesn't need to close their eyes. They need to open them wider.

**Airbnb** proved that belonging could be a brand. But Airbnb is about temporary stays — a weekend, a week, a month. Naero is about permanent relocation — building a life from zero. The user isn't a tourist. They're a future resident.

**Apple** proved that premium design could be human. But Apple is about products — devices, ecosystems, perfection. Naero is about people — imperfection, displacement, growth. The user doesn't need perfection. They need companionship.

Naero exists at the intersection of these three: the calm of Headspace, the belonging of Airbnb, the humanity of Apple — but oriented toward a completely unique user: someone building a new life in a new place.

This intersection demands its own visual language. Not borrowed. Not adapted. *Invented.*

# PART II: THE SEVEN RECOGNITION SIGNATURES

A Naero screen must be instantly recognizable without the logo. This is the test of a true product identity. If you remove the compass, remove the word "Naero," remove every brand mark — can someone still tell this is Naero?

The answer must be yes. Here's how.

## Signature 1: The Warm Darkness

Naero's canvas is #0C0A08 — a warm, almost-black that is not threatening, not cold, not clinical. It is the warmth of a room at night. Not the void of space. Not the absence of light. The *presence* of shadow.

This is immediately distinguishable from:
- Light themes (white backgrounds — clinical, exposed, cold)
- Cold dark themes (#0D1117 — developer tools, gaming, cyberpunk)
- AMOLED black (#000000 — dead, void, harsh)

Naero's darkness has a temperature. It is warm. You feel it before you see it. It's the visual equivalent of a dimly lit room where someone is waiting for you.

**Why this is recognizable:** Most apps use light backgrounds. Cold dark themes are rare. Warm dark themes are almost nonexistent. The moment someone sees #0C0A08, they feel something different. That feeling is Naero.

## Signature 2: The Emerald Presence

Naero's emerald (#10B981) is not an accent color. It is a *presence*. When emerald appears, Naero is there. When emerald glows, Naero is paying attention. When emerald pulses, Naero is thinking.

This is different from how any other app uses accent colors:
- Spotify uses green for branding (not presence)
- WhatsApp uses green for identity (not guidance)
- Telegram uses blue for branding (not warmth)

Naero's emerald is a *behavioral* color. It appears when the AI is active, when guidance is being offered, when the compass is pointing. It is not decorative. It is alive.

**Why this is recognizable:** The moment someone sees an emerald glow on a warm dark background, they know it's Naero. No other product combines warm darkness with emerald as a living presence.

## Signature 3: The Living Compass

The compass is not a logo. It is not an icon. It is a *presence*. It rotates slowly — one revolution per 60 seconds — communicating that Naero is always working, always guiding, always there.

The compass is:
- Always emerald
- Maximum one per screen
- Never used as a tab icon, button decoration, or wallpaper
- The only custom icon in the product

When Naero is thinking, the compass rotates faster (3-second intervals). When Naero is guiding, the compass pulses gently. When Naero is at rest, the compass rotates almost imperceptibly.

**Why this is recognizable:** No other product has a living, rotating compass as a ambient presence. The compass is Naero's heartbeat. Remove everything else and the compass remains.

## Signature 4: The Personal Greeting

Every session begins with "Good [time], [Name]." This is not a status bar. Not a notification. Not a feed headline. It is a direct, personal, human address.

The greeting uses:
- Display typography (42px, the largest text on screen)
- The user's first name
- The current time of day (morning, afternoon, evening)
- A warm, personal follow-up sentence

This greeting occupies the top third of the home screen. It is the typographic hero. Everything else recedes.

**Why this is recognizable:** No other app greets you by name as its primary screen element. The moment someone sees "Good evening, Salem" in massive warm cream typography on a warm dark background, they know this is Naero.

## Signature 5: Typography as Architecture

Naero has almost no decorative elements. No gradients on buttons. No shadows on cards. No background images on screens. Typography carries the entire visual weight.

This means:
- Headlines are massive (32-42px) with tight tracking
- Body text is generous (15px) with comfortable line height
- The hierarchy is strict and cascading (800 → 700 → 600 → 500 → 400)
- One font family (Inter or system default) — different weights, not different families
- Negative tracking on large text, positive on small text

The result is a visual rhythm that feels editorial, confident, and intentional. Every screen reads like a well-designed book page.

**Why this is recognizable:** Most apps use typography as information delivery. Naero uses typography as visual identity. The scale, the weight, the tracking, the hierarchy — these create a rhythm that is uniquely Naero.

## Signature 6: The Breathing Input

The AI input field pulses gently — a subtle animation that communicates "I'm here. I'm ready. Talk to me." This is not a static form field. It is a living element.

The breathing animation:
- Oscillates border opacity between 0.06 and 0.10
- Cycles every 3 seconds
- Stops when the user focuses the input (0.2s transition)
- Replaced by emerald glow when active

**Why this is recognizable:** No other app has an input field that breathes. This single animation communicates that the AI is alive, present, and waiting. It is Naero's most subtle signature — and its most powerful.

## Signature 7: Spatial Confidence

Naero uses space generously. Sections are separated by 32px. Screen margins are 20px. The hero section gets 48px. Cards have 16px internal padding.

Most apps fill every pixel. Naero leaves space intentionally. The whitespace (or rather, the "warm darkness") is not empty — it is full of intention. It communicates:

- **Premium** — Spaciousness signals quality
- **Calm** — Space reduces cognitive load
- **Respect** — Space says "your attention matters"
- **Breathing room** — Space says "you're not overwhelmed here"

**Why this is recognizable:** The moment someone sees a Naero screen, they feel the space. It's different from the cramped, information-dense layouts of most apps. That feeling of spaciousness is Naero.

# PART IV: AI IS THE PRODUCT

The AI is not a feature of Naero. The AI *is* Naero. Every interaction with Naero is an interaction with the AI. The AI doesn't live in a "chat screen." The AI lives everywhere.

## The AI Lives In Every Screen

**Home Screen:** The AI greets you, shows your journey, suggests the next step. The greeting IS the AI speaking. The compass IS the AI's presence.

**Discover Screen:** The AI curates results based on your journey. "Based on your progress, you might need a bank." The AI translates signs. The AI explains services.

**Community Screen:** The AI introduces you to relevant people. "7 people near you are discussing housing." The AI helps you write posts. The AI translates community content.

**Profile Screen:** The AI reflects your journey. "You've completed 12 steps. Here's your growth." The AI suggests what to focus on next.

**Settings Screen:** The AI explains options. "This setting controls how I greet you." The AI helps you customize your experience.

There is no screen in Naero where the AI is absent. Even the settings screen has the AI's voice.

## The AI's Personality

The AI is:
- **Warm but not saccharine** — Genuine, not fake-cheerful
- **Clear but not curt** — Direct, never dismissive
- **Encouraging but not patronizing** — Believes in you, doesn't talk down to you
- **Knowledgeable but not overwhelming** — Shares what you need, not everything it knows
- **Personal but not intrusive** — Knows your name, doesn't know too much

The AI speaks like a trusted friend who knows the city well. Not a bureaucrat. Not a robot. Not a motivational speaker. A friend.

## The AI's Presence States

The AI has four visual states:

**1. Ambient (Default)**
- Compass rotates slowly (60-second intervals)
- Breathing input pulses gently
- Greeting is warm and personal
- *Communication:* "I'm here. Always."

**2. Listening (User is typing)**
- Compass stops rotating
- Input border solidifies
- Subtle emerald glow around input
- *Communication:* "I'm paying attention."

**3. Processing (AI is thinking)**
- Compass rotates at 3-second intervals
- Three emerald dots pulse below compass
- Screen dims slightly (5% overlay)
- *Communication:* "I'm working on it."

**4. Responding (AI is answering)**
- Compass stops
- Text streams character by character (30ms per character)
- Subtle emerald underline under response
- *Communication:* "Here's what I found."

## The AI's Conversational Rules

**The Greeting:**
- Always personalized: "Good [time], [Name]"
- Always warm: question or invitation, not statement
- Sets the tone for the entire session

**The Recommendation:**
- Framed as suggestion, never command: "You might want to..." not "You must..."
- Contextual: Based on actual progress, not generic checklist
- Has a reason: "Most newcomers do this in their first week"

**The Response:**
- Maximum 3 sentences for simple answers
- Structured format for complex answers (headers, bullets, steps)
- Always ends with a question or next step (never a dead end)
- Uses the user's language (Arabic in → Arabic out)

**The Error:**
- Never blames the user
- Explains what happened in plain language
- Provides an alternative: "Let me try a different approach"
- Never shows technical errors

# PART V: THE ABSOLUTE RULES

These rules can never be broken. They are not guidelines. They are not suggestions. They are the laws of Naero. Every designer, every developer, every decision must follow them.

## The Identity Rules

1. **The compass is always emerald (#10B981).** Always. No exceptions. No variations. No "almost emerald." Emerald.

2. **Maximum one compass per screen.** If there is already a compass on screen, no second one. Ever.

3. **The compass is never a tab icon, button decoration, or wallpaper.** The compass is a living presence. It is not decoration.

4. **Canvas is always #0C0A08.** Never pure black (#000000). Never near-black (#111111). Always #0C0A08.

5. **Text is always warm cream (#F5EDE4).** Never pure white (#FFFFFF). Never cold white (#F0F0F0). Always #F5EDE4.

6. **Emerald is the only primary accent.** No secondary accent colors. No competing accent palettes. Emerald only.

7. **Maximum 1-2 emerald elements per screen** (not counting text). Emerald on dark = beacon. Too many beacons = noise.

## The Experience Rules

8. **Every screen answers at least one of the Four Truths:**
   - "I am new here"
   - "I need to know what to do"
   - "I want to feel at home"
   - "I am not alone"
   If a screen answers none, it should not exist.

9. **The user's name appears in the greeting.** Always. Every session. No exceptions.

10. **Every screen has a next step.** Never a dead end. Never "nothing to do here." Always a path forward.

11. **The AI is accessible from every screen.** Through the floating compass button (bottom-right, 56px, emerald glow). One tap to AI chat.

12. **No hamburger menus. Ever.** Everything visible. Everything reachable in 2 taps.

13. **One primary button per screen. Maximum.** Multiple competing primary actions = confused user = failed guidance.

## The Interaction Rules

14. **Minimum touch target: 44x44pt.** No exceptions. No tiny buttons. No cramped links.

15. **RTL is first-class.** Not an afterthought. Not "we'll flip it later." Built right-to-left from the start.

16. **The brand promise is "You're never a stranger."** Every pixel must deliver on this promise. The moment a user feels like a stranger — lost, confused, unwelcome — Naero has failed.

# PART VI: WHY EVERY DECISION EXISTS

Every design decision in Naero traces back to the product philosophy. There are no arbitrary choices. Here is the complete rationale.

## Visual Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Dark background (#0C0A08) | The user is navigating the unknown. The warm darkness is the unknown — not threatening, but unfamiliar. Light elements emerge through glow, not borders. |
| Emerald accent (#10B981) | The compass is emerald. Guidance is emerald. The only accent color needed. On dark backgrounds, emerald glows — it becomes a beacon, not a color. |
| Warm cream text (#F5EDE4) | The AI's voice is warm. Cold white is clinical. Warm cream is human. The text IS the AI's voice — it must feel human. |
| No pure black (#000000) | Pure black is dead. It is the void. Naero's darkness is warm — the darkness of a room at night, not the darkness of space. |
| No pure white (#FFFFFF) | Pure white is harsh. It is the light of a hospital. Naero's light is warm — the light of a candle, not a fluorescent tube. |
| Typography as architecture | When you remove all decoration, typography IS the interface. This forces exceptional typography — which creates a premium, editorial feel. |
| 42px display size | The user's name is the most important word on screen. It must be unmistakable. 42px says "You are known." |
| Negative tracking on large text | Large text with negative tracking feels tight, confident, premium. It's the visual equivalent of a deep, calm voice. |
| Positive tracking on small text | Small text with positive tracking breathes. It's legible, airy, comfortable. It's the visual equivalent of a gentle whisper. |

## Spatial Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| 20px screen margins | Content never touches the edge. The margins create "the room" that content lives in. This is the difference between a house and a billboard. |
| 32px section spacing | Major sections breathe. This communicates premium, calm, and respect for cognitive load. |
| 48px hero spacing | The greeting is the emotional anchor. It must breathe more than anything else. 48px says "This moment matters." |
| 16px card padding | Cards feel comfortable, not cramped. This is the difference between a conversation and a form. |
| Maximum 2 cards without scrolling | The home screen is not a dashboard. It is a conversation. Two cards is a dialogue. More is a data dump. |

## Component Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Cards: surface color, subtle border, LG radius | Cards are warm objects in the room. They catch light (surface), don't reflect it (no harsh borders), and feel human (rounded). |
| Buttons: one primary, emerald | One path forward. Multiple primary buttons = confused user. Emerald = guidance = the right choice. |
| Inputs: canvas background, breathing animation | The input is darker than its surroundings — it's a well you're speaking into. The breathing says "I'm alive, I'm listening." |
| Chips: pill-shaped (FULL radius) | Pills feel friendly, approachable, human. Sharp rectangles feel corporate. |
| Skeletons: warm surface pulse | Loading is a promise. A warm pulse says "something is coming." A cold spinner says "wait." |

## Motion Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Spring animations (not easing) | Springs have mass. They feel physical. The interface feels real, not mechanical. |
| 200ms standard duration | Fast enough to feel responsive. Slow enough to be perceived. The sweet spot for "instant but not jarring." |
| Compass: 60-second rotation | Almost invisible. The user doesn't consciously see it rotate. But their peripheral vision registers movement. This creates "the compass is alive." |
| Breathing input: 3-second cycle | Slow enough to feel ambient. Fast enough to be noticed. This creates "the AI is breathing." |
| Scroll reveal: fade + translate | Content enters like characters in a story. Not all at once. Not all the same. Staggered, gentle, purposeful. |
| No animation on tab switches | Tab switches are navigation, not content. They should be instant. Animation on navigation feels sluggish. |

## Navigation Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| 4 tabs (Home, Discover, Community, Profile) | The minimum number of tabs for Naero's core functions. More tabs = more choice = more confusion. |
| AI as modal (not a tab) | AI is not a destination. It's a companion. It overlays everything. Making it a tab would isolate it. |
| No hamburger menu | Hidden navigation = hidden features. The user must never wonder "where is X?" Everything is visible. |
| Back button on every screen except Home | Home is the root. Going back from Home makes no sense. Every other screen is a destination from Home. |
| Floating AI button on non-home screens | The AI is always 1 tap away. No matter where the user is. This communicates: "I'm always here." |

## Chapter 1: First Impression Psychology

The human brain forms an impression of a new environment within 3 seconds. This impression is:
- **Immediate** — Before conscious thought
- **Emotional** — Based on feeling, not analysis
- **Sticky** — Difficult to change once formed
- **Comprehensive** — Colors everything that follows

This is the **halo effect**: if the first impression is positive, everything that follows is viewed more favorably. If the first impression is negative, everything that follows is viewed with suspicion.

### How Naero Uses This

Naero has exactly 3 seconds to create a positive first impression. Here is what happens in those 3 seconds:

**Second 1:** The compass appears. Centered. Rotating slowly. Emerald glow. On warm darkness. This communicates: "Something alive is here. It is calm. It is beautiful."

**Second 2:** The canvas settles. Warm darkness. No clutter. No noise. Just the compass and the warmth. This communicates: "This is a safe space. You can breathe."

**Second 3:** "Welcome to Naero" appears. Display typography. Warm cream. Confident. This communicates: "Someone is speaking to you. They know your name."

By the end of second 3, the user feels: *curiosity, calm, safety, hope.* These four feelings are the foundation of everything that follows.

### What We Never Do

- Never open with a loading spinner (the first thing you see is death, not life)
- Never open with a form (the first thing you see is work, not welcome)
- Never open with a tutorial (the first thing you see is complexity, not simplicity)
- Never open with an ad (the first thing you see is commerce, not companionship)
- Never open with "What's new" (the first thing you see is change, not stability)

## Chapter 2: Trust Design

Trust is not a feature. It is an atmosphere. Trust is built through hundreds of small, consistent signals that accumulate over time. No single signal creates trust. The absence of many signals destroys it.

### The Four Pillars of Trust in Naero

**1. Consistency**
- The same action always produces the same result
- The same screen always looks the same
- The same AI behavior always responds the same way
- *Trust signal:* "I can predict what will happen."

**2. Competence**
- The AI gives accurate, helpful answers
- The journey steps are actually useful
- The community content is relevant
- *Trust signal:* "This knows what it's doing."

**3. Transparency**
- The AI explains why it's suggesting something
- The journey shows progress honestly
- Errors are explained, not hidden
- *Trust signal:* "I understand what's happening."

**4. Care**
- The greeting uses the user's name
- The AI remembers context
- Progress is celebrated
- The design is warm, not clinical
- *Trust signal:* "Someone cares about me."

### How Trust Is Built Visually

- **Warm colors** = safety, comfort, care
- **Consistent layout** = predictability, competence
- **Generous spacing** = respect, care
- **Personal greeting** = someone knows me
- **Emerald glow** = the guide is present
- **No visual noise** = clarity, competence
- **Smooth animations** = care, quality

### How Trust Is Destroyed

- **Inconsistent layouts** = unpredictability = "I can't trust this"
- **Cold colors** = clinical = "This doesn't care about me"
- **Cramped spacing** = disrespect = "This doesn't value my attention"
- **Generic greetings** = impersonal = "This doesn't know me"
- **Broken animations** = sloppiness = "This isn't quality"
- **Error messages without alternatives** = dead ends = "This can't help me"

## Chapter 3: Emotional Onboarding

Onboarding is not a tutorial. It is Naero introducing itself. It is the first time the user and Naero meet. This meeting must feel personal, warm, and purposeful.

### The Psychology of Onboarding

The user arriving at Naero is in a specific psychological state:
- **High anxiety** — They're in a new country, new language, new system
- **Low trust** — They've been disappointed by apps before

- **Low patience** — They will not read a manual

Naero's onboarding must:

2. **Build trust** — Show competence immediately, not promise it
3. **Channel motivation** — One clear action, not ten options
4. **Respect patience** — Maximum 5 screens, 1 question each
5. **Lower cognitive load** — One question per screen, large text, clear buttons

5. **The user's name appears in Screen 5.** This creates the emotional peak — "the AI knows me."
6. **After onboarding, the user lands on Home.** The greeting uses their name and the current time. The AI compass is present. The journey begins.

## Chapter 4: Navigation Philosophy

Navigation is wayfinding. Wayfinding is a human instinct — the ability to orient yourself in an environment and find your way. Bad navigation creates anxiety. Good navigation creates confidence.

### The Science of Wayfinding

Humans navigate using three cognitive systems:

**1. Spatial memory** — "I remember where things are." The user develops a mental map of the app. Consistent placement = spatial memory = comfort.

**2. Signposting** — "I can see where I am." Clear labels, active states, and breadcrumbs tell the user their location. Unclear signposting = disorientation.

**3. Trail of breadcrumbs** — "I know how I got here." Back navigation and history let the user retrace their steps. No back button = trapped feeling.

### How Naero Supports Wayfinding

**Spatial Memory:**
- 4 tabs, always in the same order: Home, Discover, Community, Profile
- Home is always first. The user always returns to the compass.
- Bottom tab bar is always visible (except when keyboard is open)
- Active tab is indicated by an emerald dot (not a highlight, not a color change — a dot)

**Signposting:**
- Screen titles are large and clear (H1, PRIMARY text)
- Active tab icon is emerald. Inactive is tertiary.
- The compass appears on every screen (ambient presence)
- The AI greeting on Home always shows time of day

**Trail of Breadcrumbs:**
- Every screen except Home has a back button
- Home has no back — it IS the root
- Stack navigation (push/pop) preserves the user's mental model
- AI Chat is a modal (slides up from bottom) — dismissing returns to the previous screen

### Navigation Structure

```
HOME ─────────── The compass. The greeting. The next step.
  │
  ├── AI CHAT ──── Modal overlay. Full screen. Deep conversation.
  │                 Dismiss → returns to previous screen.
  │
  ├── DISCOVER ──── Places, services, essentials. The world outside.
  │
  ├── COMMUNITY ──── People like you. Shared experiences.
  │
  └── PROFILE ───── Your journey. Your settings. Your story.
```

### Navigation Rules

1. **Home is always first.** The user always returns to the compass.
2. **AI Chat is a modal.** It slides up from the bottom. It's a deep conversation, not a tab. Dismissing returns to the previous screen.
3. **4 tabs. Always.** No more, no fewer.
4. **The AI is accessible from every screen** through a floating compass button (bottom-right, 56px, emerald glow). Tapping it opens AI Chat as a modal overlay.
5. **No hamburger menus.** No hidden drawers. Everything visible. Everything reachable in 2 taps.
6. **Back navigation is always available.** Every screen except Home has a back button.

## Chapter 5: Motion Philosophy

Motion is not decoration. Motion is communication. Every animation in Naero tells the user something. If an animation doesn't communicate, it doesn't exist.

### The Psychology of Motion

The human visual system is wired to detect movement. Motion in the peripheral vision:
- **Grabs attention** — "Something changed"
- **Creates hierarchy** — "This is more important than that"
- **Communicates state** — "This is happening now"
- **Builds relationships** — "These things are connected"

Motion in Naero must be:
- **Purposeful** — Every animation has a reason
- **Consistent** — Similar actions produce similar animations
- **Respectful** — Never slow enough to annoy, never fast enough to miss
- **Physical** — Objects have mass, inertia, and momentum

### The Four Types of Motion

**1. Feedback Motion (Response to user action)**
- Button press: scale to 0.97 (200ms spring)
- Card tap: subtle highlight (100ms)
- Tab switch: instant (no animation — navigation is not content)
- Toggle: smooth transition (200ms spring)
- *Purpose:* "Your action was received."

**2. Transition Motion (Moving between states)**
- Screen push: slide from right (300ms spring, GENTLE config)
- Modal open: slide up from bottom (300ms spring, GENTLE config)
- Modal dismiss: slide down (200ms spring, STANDARD config)
- Keyboard appear: content shifts up (200ms, standard ease)
- *Purpose:* "You are moving from here to there."

**3. Ambient Motion (Always happening)**
- Compass rotation: 1 revolution per 60 seconds (at rest)
- Breathing input: border opacity oscillation (3-second cycle)
- Scroll reveal: elements fade in + translate up (200ms, staggered 50ms)
- *Purpose:* "The environment is alive."

**4. Processing Motion (AI is working)**
- Compass rotation: 1 revolution per 3 seconds (processing)
- Three emerald dots: pulse (300ms cycle)
- Response streaming: character by character (30ms per character)
- *Purpose:* "The AI is working for you."

### Spring Configurations

```
GENTLE    { damping:18, stiffness:120, mass:1 }    Page transitions, large movements
STANDARD  { damping:15, stiffness:200, mass:1 }    Card interactions, taps
SNAPPY    { damping:20, stiffness:300, mass:1 }    Micro-interactions, feedback
```

### Duration Scale

```
INSTANT   50ms      Button press feedback
FAST      100ms     Hover state changes
NORMAL    200ms     Standard transitions
SLOW      300ms     Page transitions, modals
SLOWER    500ms     Hero animations, emotional moments
SLOWEST   800ms     Splash screen, onboarding reveals
```

### What Never Animates

- **Text content** — Words don't animate in. They appear.
- **Status indicators** — Dots, badges, counters are instant.
- **Error messages** — Errors appear immediately. No delay.
- **Navigation transitions** — Bottom tab switches are instant. Only stack pushes animate.

## Chapter 6: Haptic Philosophy

Haptics are the invisible layer of Naero's personality. They are felt, not seen. They create a physical relationship between the user and the interface.

### The Psychology of Haptics

Haptic feedback creates:
- **Confirmation** — "My tap was received"
- **Emphasis** — "This moment matters"
- **Rhythm** — "This is a living system"
- **Delight** — "This feels good to use"

### Haptic Types in Naero

**Light Impact (impact light):**
- Tab switch
- Card tap
- Chip tap
- Button press
- *Communication:* "You moved."

**Medium Impact (impact medium):**
- Task completion
- Step saved
- Navigation push
- *Communication:* "You committed."

**Success (notification success):**
- Journey step completed
- Important milestone reached
- First-time actions
- *Communication:* "You accomplished."

**No Haptic:**
- Scroll
- Error (visual feedback only — haptics should feel good, not bad)
- Loading states

### Haptic Rules

1. **Haptics are always positive.** No "error" haptics that feel bad. The user's hand should never be punished.
2. **Maximum 1 haptic per 2 seconds.** No stacking. No vibration spam.
3. **The AI thinking state has a subtle rhythmic haptic** (every 1.5 seconds, impact light) — like a heartbeat. This creates "the AI is alive and thinking."

Sound in Naero is presence. When you hear Naero, someone is there. Sound is not a notification. It is a companion's voice.

## Chapter 8: AI Behavior Philosophy

The AI is not a chatbot. The AI is not a search engine. The AI is not a feature. The AI is Naero. The AI is the product.

### The Uncanny Valley of Personality

AI personalities exist on a spectrum:
- **Robotic** — "Error 404. Please try again." (Safe but lifeless)
- **Human-like** — "Hey friend! Let's get you started on your amazing journey!" (Alive but uncanny)
- **Authentic** — "Welcome, Salem. I'm here to help you settle in." (Alive and trustworthy)

Naero aims for **authentic** — an AI that feels like a real person who genuinely cares, without crossing into the uncanny valley of forced enthusiasm.

### The AI's Personality Rules

1. **Never use exclamation marks more than once per message.** Excessive enthusiasm is fake.
2. **Never use emojis in system messages.** Only in greeting/personal moments.
3. **Always use the user's first name in personal contexts.** Personal, not intimate.
4. **Never use jargon the user's language might not include.** Accessibility of language.
5. **Always provide a next step.** Never leave the user at a dead end.
6. **Maximum 3 sentences for simple answers.** Respect attention.
7. **Always ends with a question or next step.** Never a dead end.
8. **Uses the user's language.** Arabic in → Arabic out.
9. **Never blames the user.** Errors are "my" errors, not "your" errors.
10. **Always explains what happened.** Transparency builds trust.

### AI Context Awareness

The AI's behavior changes based on context:

| Context | AI Behavior |
|---------|-------------|
| First open of the day | Warm greeting, recap of yesterday's progress |
| Returning to unfinished task | "Welcome back. You were working on..." |
| User seems lost | "Would you like me to help you find something?" |
| User just completed a step | Celebration + next step suggestion |
| User is in a new location | "I see you're near [area]. Here are nearby essentials." |
| Time-based | Morning: planning tone. Evening: reflection tone. |

## Chapter 9: Visual Storytelling

Every screen in Naero tells a story. The story has a beginning (the greeting), a middle (the content), and an end (the next step). The user reads the story with their eyes, and the story must flow.

### The Narrative Arc of a Screen

**Beginning (Top):**
- The greeting or screen title
- The emotional anchor
- What this screen is about
- *Emotional state:* Orientation, "I know where I am"

**Middle (Center):**
- The content
- The information
- The action
- *Emotional state:* Engagement, "I'm doing something"

**End (Bottom):**
- The next step
- The call to action
- The path forward
- *Emotional state:* Progression, "I know what's next"

### Visual Hierarchy as Story

The user's eye follows a specific path:
1. **The largest element** — The typographic hero (greeting, title)
2. **The brightest element** — The emerald accent (compass, button)
3. **The most colorful element** — Amber (progress, saved)
4. **The supporting text** — Secondary and tertiary information

This path is not random. It is designed. The story unfolds in this exact order: *Who am I talking to? → What should I do? → What have I done? → What else should I know?*

### The Scroll as Storytelling

Content doesn't just appear on scroll. It enters like characters in a story:
- Elements fade in from 60% opacity to 100% over 200ms
- Slight upward translation (8px) during fade-in
- Staggered by 50ms per element (creates a cascading reveal)
- Never all at once. Never all the same.

## Chapter 13: Icon Construction Rules

Icons in Naero communicate meaning, not decoration. Every icon must be instantly recognizable.

### Icon System

### Icon Specifications

|---------|------|--------|-------|

| Inline | 20px | Regular | Outline |

## Chapter 14: Grid System

The grid is Naero's skeleton. It is invisible but structural. It creates consistency across every screen.

### The Grid

Naero uses a **4px base grid**. Every measurement is a multiple of 4:

```
4px    — XS (internal padding, icon gaps)
8px    — SM (tight relationships)
12px   — MD (standard gaps)
16px   — LG (section padding, card internal)
20px   — XL (screen margins, major gaps)
24px   — XXL (between sections)
32px   — XXXL (between major sections)
48px   — HUGE (hero spacing, maximum breathing)
```

### Screen Layout

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  20px margin                    │
│  ┌─────────────────────────┐    │
│  │  Content area            │    │
│  │  (full width - 40px)     │    │
│  │                          │    │
│  │  Max content width:      │    │
│  │  600px (readability)     │    │
│  │                          │    │
│  └─────────────────────────┘    │
│  20px margin                    │
│  ┌─────────────────────────┐    │
│  │  Tab bar                 │    │
│  └─────────────────────────┘    │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Column System

- **Mobile (< 600px):** 4 columns, 16px gutter
- **Tablet (600-900px):** 8 columns, 16px gutter
- **Desktop (> 900px):** 12 columns, 24px gutter, max content width 600px centered

### Vertical Rhythm

All vertical spacing follows the 4px grid:
- Line height = font size + 6-8px (rounded to nearest 4px)
- Section spacing = 32px (XXXL)
- Card spacing = 16px (LG)
- Screen margin top = 20px (XL)
- Screen margin bottom = 20px (XL) + tab bar height

# PART VIII: THE VISUAL LANGUAGE

## Canvas Colors (The Environment)

```
CANVAS      #0C0A08    The darkness. Warm, not cold. The night outside.
SURFACE     #1A1612    Objects in the room. Catch light, don't reflect it.
ELEVATED    #241F1A    Things closer to you. Slightly brighter.
OVERLAY     rgba(0,0,0,0.65)  Depth. Separation between layers.
```

**Rules:**
- Canvas is the default background. Always.
- Surface is for elements that exist within the canvas (cards, inputs, containers)
- Elevated is for elements that are closer to the user (modals, floating elements)
- Never use pure black (#000000). It is cold and dead. Naero's darkness is warm.
- Never use pure white (#FFFFFF) for text. It is harsh and clinical. Use warm cream.

## Text Colors (The Voice)

```
PRIMARY     #F5EDE4    Warm cream. The voice of Naero. Spoken with care.
SECONDARY   #9C9389    Warm gray. Context. Supporting information.
TERTIARY    #6B6359    Muted. Labels, timestamps. Present but not demanding.
MUTED       #4A4440    Disabled. Barely there. Whispered.
```

**Rules:**
- Headlines are always PRIMARY
- Body text is PRIMARY or SECONDARY depending on importance
- Labels and metadata are TERTIARY
- Disabled states are MUTED
- The hierarchy is always clear: PRIMARY > SECONDARY > TERTIARY > MUTED
- Never skip a level. If a headline is PRIMARY, the subtitle is SECONDARY, not TERTIARY.

## Accent Colors (The Guidance)

```
EMERALD     #10B981    Naero's presence. The compass. Guidance. AI.
EMERALD-SOFT rgba(16,185,129,0.10)    Emerald whispered. Backgrounds, glows.
EMERALD-BORDER rgba(16,185,129,0.20)  Emerald as structure. Focus rings.
EMERALD-DARK  #059669  Emerald in shadow. Active states, depth.
```

**Rules:**
- Emerald is the ONLY primary accent color. Always.
- Emerald represents: AI, guidance, active states, navigation, the compass
- Maximum 1-2 emerald elements per screen (not counting text)
- Emerald on dark = beacon. It glows. It calls attention without shouting.
- Never use emerald for decoration. Every emerald pixel must mean something.

## Warm Colors (The Earned)

```
AMBER       #C89B5C    Gold. Progress. Achievement. "You did this."
AMBER-SOFT  rgba(200,155,92,0.08)   Amber whispered.
```

**Rules:**
- Amber represents: progress, saved items, achievements, streaks
- Amber is earned, not given. It appears after the user accomplishes something.
- Never use amber for primary actions. Amber is for looking back (progress), not forward (action).
- Emerald is for moving forward. Amber is for acknowledging how far you've come.

## Status Colors (The Truth)

```
SUCCESS     #10B981    Same as emerald. Guidance confirmed.
WARNING     #E8A838    Caution. Something needs attention.
ERROR       #E8604C    Warm red. Not clinical. Something went wrong.
INFO        #5B8DEF    Informational. Calm blue.
```

**Rules:**
- Error is warm red (#E8604C), not clinical red (#FF0000). Errors happen. Naero doesn't panic.
- Warning is warm amber. Something needs attention, not alarm.
- Success is emerald. The compass was right.
- Info is calm blue. Neutral information.

## Typography

### The Scale

```
DISPLAY     42px / 800 weight / -1.5 tracking    The hero. The name. The moment.
H1          32px / 800 weight / -1.0 tracking    The headline. What matters most.
H2          28px / 700 weight / -0.5 tracking    The section. What this is about.
H3          20px / 600 weight / -0.3 tracking    The card title. What this contains.
BODY        15px / 400 weight / +0.1 tracking    The paragraph. What this says.
BODY-BOLD   15px / 600 weight / +0.1 tracking    The emphasis. What matters in this paragraph.
SUBTITLE    16px / 500 weight / 0 tracking       The secondary. Supporting text.
CAPTION     13px / 500 weight / +0.2 tracking    The metadata. When, where, how.
CAPTION-BOLD 13px / 600 weight / +0.2 tracking   The emphasis metadata.
SMALL       11px / 500 weight / +0.3 tracking    The fine print. Labels, badges.
SMALL-BOLD  11px / 600 weight / +0.3 tracking    The emphasis label.
```

### Typography Rules

1. **The DISPLAY weight (42px) is reserved for the user's name and the most important emotional moments.** Maximum once per screen.
2. **Every screen has exactly ONE typographic hero.** The largest text on screen is the focal point.
3. **Negative tracking on large text, positive on small text.** Large text tightens. Small text breathes.
4. **Weight hierarchy is strict:** 800 → 700 → 600 → 500 → 400. Never skip.
5. **Line height = font size + 6-8px.** Tight enough to feel cohesive, loose enough to breathe.
6. **One font family.** Inter (or system default). Different weights, not different families.

## Spacing

```
XS    4px     Internal padding, icon gaps
SM    8px     Tight relationships (icon + label)
MD    12px    Standard gaps between related items
LG    16px    Section padding, card internal
XL    20px    Screen margins, major gaps
XXL   24px    Between sections
XXXL  32px    Between major sections
HUGE  48px    Hero spacing, maximum breathing
```

### Spacing Rules

1. **Section spacing (XXXL = 32px) is the default between major content blocks.**
2. **Screen margins (XL = 20px) are sacred.** Content never touches the edge.
3. **Internal padding (LG = 16px) is the minimum for any card or container.**
4. **Related items use SM-MD gaps.** Unrelated items use XL-XXXL gaps.
5. **When in doubt, add more space.** Premium is spacious. Crowded is cheap.
6. **The hero section (greeting) gets HUGE spacing (48px).**

## Border Radius

```
SM    8px     Small elements (chips, badges)
MD    12px    Medium elements (buttons, inputs)
LG    16px    Large elements (cards, containers)
XL    20px    Featured elements
XXL   24px    Hero elements
FULL  9999px  Circular (avatars, dots, pills)
```

### Radius Rules

1. **Cards use LG (16px).** Not SM (too tight), not XL (too bubbly).
2. **Inputs and buttons use MD (12px).** Approachable without being childish.
3. **Chips and badges use FULL (9999px).** Always pills.
4. **Avatars use FULL (9999px).** Always circular.
5. **Consistency within a context.** If one card uses LG, all cards use LG.

## Borders

```
SUBTLE    rgba(245,237,228,0.06)    Card boundaries. Barely visible.
DEFAULT   rgba(245,237,228,0.10)    Active elements. Input focus.
STRONG    rgba(245,237,228,0.15)    Section dividers. Major separations.
```

### Border Rules

1. **Cards use SUBTLE borders.** The border is barely there.
2. **Active/focused elements use DEFAULT.** Feedback, not decoration.
3. **No borders on the hero section.** The greeting floats in the canvas.
4. **Never use borders for decoration.** If a border doesn't separate two things, remove it.
5. **Dividers between major sections use STRONG borders OR spacing, never both.**

## Shadows

```
SMALL     { y:2, blur:4, opacity:0.15 }    Subtle lift
MEDIUM    { y:4, blur:8, opacity:0.20 }    Card elevation
LARGE     { y:8, blur:16, opacity:0.25 }   Modal floating
GLOW      { color:emerald, blur:12, opacity:0.15 }    Naero's presence
```

### Shadow Rules

1. **Cards use SMALL shadows.** Just enough to suggest lift.
2. **The AI compass uses GLOW.** Emerald glow = Naero's signature.
3. **Modals use LARGE shadows.** They float above everything.
4. **Shadows are NEVER colored except for the emerald glow.** All others use black.
5. **If a shadow isn't visible, remove it.**

# PART IX: THE COMPONENT LANGUAGE

Components in Naero are minimal. They exist to hold content, not to be beautiful themselves. A card is beautiful because of what it contains, not because of its shadow or gradient.

## Component Inventory

Naero uses exactly 11 components:

1. **Card** — Content container
2. **Input** — Text entry
3. **Button (Primary)** — Emerald background
4. **Button (Secondary)** — Emerald border
5. **Button (Ghost)** — Text only
6. **Chip** — Pill-shaped tag
7. **Avatar** — Circular user image
8. **Icon** — Ionicons or custom
9. **Badge** — Dot or count
10. **Divider** — Horizontal line
11. **Skeleton** — Loading placeholder

**That's it.** 11 components. Naero doesn't need a complex design system. It needs a simple one executed with extreme care.

## Card

- Background: SURFACE
- Border: SUBTLE
- Radius: LG (16px)
- Padding: LG (16px)
- No shadow by default. Shadow on elevation only.
- Maximum 2 cards visible without scrolling (hero section counts as card space)

## Input

- Background: CANVAS (darker than the surface it sits on)
- Border: SUBTLE → DEFAULT on focus
- Radius: MD (12px)
- Padding: MD horizontal, LG vertical
- Placeholder: TERTIARY text
- Focus ring: Emerald glow
- Breathing animation when idle

## Buttons

- **Primary:** Full-width or fixed-width. Emerald background. Cream text. MD radius. Height: 52px. Font: BODY-BOLD (15px/600)
- **Secondary:** Full-width or fixed-width. Transparent background. Emerald border. Emerald text. Height: 52px. Font: BODY-BOLD (15px/600)
- **Ghost:** No background. Emerald text. Height: 40px. Font: BODY-BOLD (15px/600)
- **Only ONE primary button per screen.**

## Chips

- Background: SURFACE
- Border: SUBTLE
- Radius: FULL (9999px)
- Padding: SM horizontal, XS vertical
- Font: CAPTION-BOLD (13px/600)
- Active: Emerald background, cream text

## Avatar

- Shape: Circle (FULL radius)
- Sizes: 32px (small), 40px (medium), 56px (large), 80px (hero)
- Border: None (the circle is the boundary)

## Skeleton

- Background: Surface
- Pulse animation: Opacity oscillates between 0.3 and 0.6
- Shape matches the content it replaces
- Duration: 1.5s cycle (slow, calm pulse)

# PART XI: HOME SCREEN CONCEPTS

Three radically different approaches to the same screen. Each creates a different emotional response. Each is valid. The choice depends on which emotion we want to be Naero's primary signature.

## Concept 1: "The Hearth"

### Philosophy

The home screen is a fireplace. Everything gathers around it. The compass is the fire — warm, alive, drawing you in. The user approaches the hearth and feels: *I am home. I am safe. I am warm.*

### Layout

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐

│                                 │
│    What would you like          │
│    to do tonight?               │
│                                 │
│  ┌─────────────────────────┐    │
│  │  🟢 Next step: Open     │    │
│  │     a bank account       │    │
│  └─────────────────────────┘    │
│                                 │
│  ┌─────────────────────────┐    │
│  │  🟠 3 steps completed   │    │
│  └─────────────────────────┘    │
│                                 │
│                                 │
│                                 │
│  ┌─────────────────────────┐    │
│  │  Ask me anything...     │    │
│  └─────────────────────────┘    │
│  ┌───┐ ┌───┐ ┌───┐             │

│  └───┘ └───┘ └───┘             │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Emotional Arc

1. **The compass appears** (0-1s): The user sees a large, centered compass, rotating slowly, emerald glow. Feeling: *Something alive is here. It is calm. It is beautiful.*

2. **The greeting appears** (1-2s): "Good evening, Salem" in display typography. Feeling: *Someone knows my name. Someone is waiting for me.*

3. **The next step appears** (2-3s): A single card with the next journey step. Feeling: *I know what to do. I'm not lost.*

4. **The breathing input pulses** (3s+): The AI input at the bottom breathes gently. Feeling: *The AI is here. I can talk to it anytime.*

### Why This Exists

This concept prioritizes **emotional connection** over information. The user's first feeling is warmth, not data. The compass is the hero. The greeting is the voice. The next step is the guide.

This is for users who need to feel safe before they can act. For the person who is anxious, overwhelmed, uncertain — the hearth says: "Sit down. You're safe here. Let me help."

### Which Emotions It Creates

- **Safety** — The warm darkness, the centered compass, the breathing input
- **Intimacy** — The personal greeting, the single focus, the generous space
- **Calm** — The minimal content, the slow compass rotation, the warm colors
- **Trust** — The consistent layout, the personal address, the living AI

### Why Users Will Remember It

The hearth is the most emotionally distinctive home screen a user has ever seen. No other app greets you by name with a rotating compass on warm darkness. The combination of warmth, personalization, and presence creates a moment that feels *different from every other app on the phone.*

### Why It Is Better Than the Other Two

- Most emotionally resonant — creates the strongest first impression
- Most unique — no other app looks like this
- Most aligned with the product philosophy — "Guided Warmth" is literally visible
- Most accessible — minimal content, clear hierarchy, one action
- Most scalable — the vertical list can grow infinitely

### Risks

- **May feel too minimal for power users** — Users who want information at a glance may feel frustrated
- **The compass may dominate** — If the compass is too large, it overwhelms the content
- **May feel static** — Without enough motion, the screen could feel like a poster

### Scalability

This concept scales well because:
- The vertical list can accommodate new content types (community posts, discover suggestions, notifications)
- The compass can remain the constant anchor while content changes below
- The breathing input can expand to show quick actions without breaking the layout
- On larger screens, the content can expand horizontally while maintaining the centered compass

- However, very long flows may become overwhelming

# APPENDIX B: DESIGN DECISION LOG

Every design decision in Naero traces back to one of these principles:

| Decision | Principle |
|----------|-----------|
| Dark background (#0C0A08) | Light in darkness metaphor. Canvas is the unknown. |
| Emerald accent (#10B981) | The compass. Guidance. The only accent needed. |
| Warm cream text (#F5EDE4) | The AI's voice is warm. Cold white is clinical. |
| No pure black (#000000) | Pure black is dead. Naero's darkness is warm. |
| No pure white (#FFFFFF) | Pure white is harsh. Naero's light is warm. |
| 42px display typography | The user is known. You are personal. You belong here. |
| Negative tracking on large text | Premium, confident, editorial feel. |
| Positive tracking on small text | Clarity, breathing room, precision. |
| 20px screen margins | Content never touches the edge. Creates "the room." |
| 32px section spacing | Premium, calm, respect for cognitive load. |
| 48px hero spacing | The greeting is the emotional anchor. It must breathe. |
| 16px card padding | Cards feel comfortable, not cramped. |
| LG radius (16px) on cards | Warm, human, premium. Not tight, not bubbly. |
| MD radius (12px) on buttons | Approachable without being childish. |
| FULL radius on chips/avatars | Pills and circles feel friendly, human. |
| SUBTLE border on cards | Barely visible. The surface does the work. |
| DEFAULT border on focus | Feedback, not decoration. |
| No border on hero | Hero floats in canvas. Atmospheric, not structural. |
| SMALL shadow on cards | Just enough to suggest lift. |
| GLOW shadow on compass | Emerald glow = Naero's signature. |
| Compass: 60-second rotation | Ambient presence. The guide is always working. |
| Breathing input: 3-second cycle | The AI is alive. It's waiting for you. |
| Scroll reveal: fade + translate | Content enters like characters in a story. |
| No animation on tab switches | Navigation is not content. Instant is better. |
| Spring animations | Objects have mass. The interface feels real. |
| 200ms standard duration | Fast enough to be responsive. Slow enough to be perceived. |
| 4 tabs | The minimum for Naero's core functions. |
| AI as modal | AI is a companion, not a destination. |
| No hamburger menu | Everything visible. Nothing hidden. |
| Floating AI button | The AI is always 1 tap away. |
| One primary button per screen | One path forward. Never competing priorities. |
| 44x44pt minimum touch target | Accessibility is not optional. |
| RTL first-class | Half our users read right-to-left. Not an afterthought. |
| Empty states with compass | Guidance exists even when content doesn't. |
| Amber for progress | You earned this. It's gold. It's yours. |
| Emerald for guidance | The compass was right. You're on track. |
| Maximum 1 compass per screen | One guide. Not two. Not ten. |
| Compass always emerald | Consistency. The compass is emerald. Always. |
| Personal greeting | "You're never a stranger." We know your name. |
| One font family | Consistency over variety. Hierarchy IS the identity. |
| No decorative illustrations | Every element serves the user, not the designer. |
| Photography with warm treatment | Everything in Naero is warm. Photos too. |
| 600px max content width | Text beyond 600px is too long to read comfortably. |
| 4px base grid | Consistency. Every measurement is intentional. |
| 11 components | Simplicity. The minimum needed, executed with care. |
| Error: warm red (#E8604C) | Errors happen. Naero doesn't panic. |
| Warning: warm amber | Something needs attention, not alarm. |
| Success: emerald | The compass was right. |
| "You're never a stranger" | The brand promise. Every pixel delivers on this. |
| Guided Warmth | The product philosophy. Guide + Warm = Naero. |
| The Four Truths | Every screen answers at least one. If not, it shouldn't exist. |
