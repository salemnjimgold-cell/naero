# Jobs & Employment Guide for Hungary

## Job Market Overview

### Key Industries
- Information Technology: strong demand, English-friendly
- Finance and Banking: good opportunities in Budapest
- Automotive manufacturing: Audi, Mercedes, Suzuki
- Pharmaceuticals and biotechnology
- Customer service and shared services (SSC)
- Tourism and hospitality
- Education (especially English teaching)

### Job Search Platforms
- Profession.hu - largest Hungarian job portal
- LinkedIn - popular for international roles
- Állásbörze - job fair and portal
- NoFluffJobs - IT and tech focused
- Glassdoor - salary information
- Career.hu - general jobs

## Work Culture

### Working Hours
- Standard: 40 hours/week (8 hours/day, Monday-Friday)
- Flexible hours common in tech and creative industries
- Remote work increasingly available, especially post-COVID

### Probation Period
- Typically 90 days
- Can be extended up to 180 days by agreement
- During probation: 15-day notice period from either side

### Holidays and Leave
- Annual leave: minimum 20 days per year + additional days based on age
- Public holidays: 14 per year
- Sick leave: 15 days paid at 70% by employer, then by health insurance
- Maternity leave: 24 weeks paid
- Parental leave: up to 3 years (GYED)

## Salary Information

### Average Monthly Salaries (2024, gross)
- IT/Software Developer: 800,000 - 1,500,000 HUF
- Customer Service: 400,000 - 600,000 HUF
- Finance/Accounting: 600,000 - 1,200,000 HUF
- Marketing: 500,000 - 900,000 HUF
- Teaching English: 300,000 - 500,000 HUF
- Minimum wage: 266,800 HUF (2024)

### Salary Expectations
- 13th-month salary common in some sectors
- Bonuses: typically 1-3 months salary annually
- Gross vs net: employer pays ~50% overhead on gross salary

## CV Standards
- Include a professional photo (Hungarian standard)
- Europass format is widely accepted
- Include: personal details, education, work experience, language skills, references
- Length: 1-2 pages
- Languages: Hungarian and English for international companies

## Work Permits
- EU/EEA citizens: no permit needed
- Non-EU citizens: employer must obtain work permit
- EU Blue Card: for highly skilled workers (university degree + higher salary threshold)
- White Card: for IT professionals
- Student work: up to 24 hours/week allowed
