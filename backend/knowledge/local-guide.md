# Local Guide to Life in Hungary

## Banking

### Opening a Bank Account
- Required documents: passport, residence permit, proof of address
- Popular banks: OTP, K&H, Erste, UniCredit, Revolut (online-only)
- Account opening can take 1-2 hours
- Monthly fees: 300-1,000 HUF for basic accounts
- Online banking available in English at major banks

## Transportation

### Public Transport in Budapest
- Metro lines: M1 (yellow), M2 (red), M3 (blue), M4 (green)
- Trams, buses, trolleybuses, suburban railway (HÉV)
- Tickets: single ticket 350 HUF, monthly pass 9,500 HUF
- BKK app for ticket purchase
- Validate tickets before travel (failure = fine of 25,000 HUF)

### Driving
- Hungarian driving license accepted for EU citizens
- Non-EU citizens: exchange within 1 year
- Speed limits: 50 km/h city, 90 km/h country, 130 km/h highway
- Toll system: e-vignette required for highways (autópálya)

## Communication

### Mobile Phone
- Major providers: Telekom, Vodafone, Yettel
- Prepaid SIM: 2,000-5,000 HUF available at post offices, grocery stores
- Monthly plans: 5,000-15,000 HUF with data
- ID required for SIM registration

### Internet
- Fiber optic widely available in cities
- Monthly cost: 5,000-10,000 HUF
- Providers: Telekom, UPC, Digi, Invitech

## Daily Life

### Grocery Shopping
- Major chains: Spar, Tesco, Lidl, Aldi, Auchan, Penny Market
- Open: Mon-Sat 6:00-20:00, Sun 8:00-18:00
- Local markets (piac): fresh produce, meat, cheese
- Hungarian specialties: paprika, salami, goose liver, Tokaji wine

### Restaurants and Dining
- Lunch menu (ebéd menü): 1,500-3,000 HUF on weekdays
- Dinner at mid-range restaurant: 5,000-10,000 HUF/person
- Tip: 10-15% standard, check if service charge is included
- Tap water is safe to drink
