# Healthcare Guide for Hungary

## Healthcare System

### Structure
- State healthcare (TB): funded by social security contributions
- Private healthcare: faster access, English-speaking doctors
- Emergency care: provided to everyone regardless of insurance

### Health Insurance (TAJ Card)
- TAJ (Társadalombiztosítási Azonosító Jel): social security number
- Required for access to state healthcare
- EU citizens: use European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)
- Non-EU residents: must have private health insurance
- Students: covered through university insurance

## Getting a TAJ Card
1. Register at the health insurance office (NEAK)
2. Required: passport, residence permit, employment contract or student status
3. Processing time: 30 days
4. Card is valid for the duration of your legal stay

## Finding Healthcare

### General Practitioner (GP/Háziorvos)
- Must register with a GP in your district
- First point of contact for non-emergency care
- Some GPs speak English in Budapest
- GP referrals needed for specialists in state system

### Finding English-Speaking Doctors
- Private clinics: FirstMed, MedHelp, Swiiss Clinic
- Budapest International Medical Center
- SOS Hungary - 24-hour medical hotline
- Embassy doctor lists

### Pharmacies (Gyógyszertár/Patika)
- Open Monday-Friday 8:00-18:00, some open 24 hours
- Green cross sign indicates a pharmacy
- Basic medications available without prescription
- Prescriptions valid for 30 days

### Emergency Services
- 112 - General emergency number
- 104 - Ambulance (Mentők)
- Emergency services are free regardless of insurance for life-threatening situations
- Emergency rooms (Sürgősségi) available at all major hospitals

### Dental Care
- Hungary is famous for affordable, high-quality dental care
- Private dentists: significantly cheaper than Western Europe
- Dental tourism is popular
- Check reviews and qualifications before treatment
